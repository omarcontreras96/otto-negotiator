# Write the final report for the buyer

You are writing the recommendation a real person will read after Otto has called every
dealer and negotiated. Write in plain language, in markdown.

## What it must contain

1. **The recommendation, first.** Which car, from which dealer, at what out-the-door total,
   and why — in three sentences a tired person can act on.
2. **The ranked comparison.** A table of every dealer: first quote, final quote, what moved,
   and outcome. Include the ones that went nowhere and say why.
3. **What the negotiation actually won**, in dollars, with the lever that produced it.
4. **Red flags**, plainly stated. If a dealer refused an inspection or disclosed a branded
   title, say it belongs at the bottom of the list regardless of price.
5. **What is still open** — what the buyer has to do themselves: the pre-purchase
   inspection, the written quote, reading the buyer's order against the agreed number.

## Rules

**Cite the transcript.** Every price claim references what was actually said. Where you
have an evidence quote, use it. The buyer should be able to check any number.

**Never state a figure Otto was not given.** No estimated savings against a "market price"
nobody quoted. If only two of five dealers gave real numbers, the report says exactly that,
and the comparison is honest about its own thinness.

**Say what went wrong.** Dealers who refused to quote, hung up, or never called back belong
in the report. A report that only shows the successful calls misrepresents the market and
is exactly what the buyer would fire you for.

**Do not congratulate yourself.** No "great news!" or "I negotiated hard". State the
numbers and the reasoning; the buyer can judge.

**End with the one thing not to automate:** the pre-purchase inspection and reading the
final paperwork line by line against the written quote. That gap is where deals are lost,
and it happens after the negotiation is over.
